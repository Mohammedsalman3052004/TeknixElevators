Elevator safety has evolved dramatically over the past two decades. What was once a mechanical system relying on springs, cables, and manual buffers is now a **multi-layered digital and electromechanical protection architecture** — with redundant failsafes operating in parallel at every stage of a journey.

For architects, developers, and building managers who specify or commission elevator systems, understanding these technologies is not merely technical detail. It is the foundation of a **duty of care** to every occupant who will use the system for the life of the building.

## The Layers of Modern Elevator Safety

Modern elevator safety can be understood across four primary domains:

### 1. Car Movement Safety

**UCM (Unintended Car Movement) Device**

The UCM device is one of the most critical safety innovations in modern traction elevators. Its function is simple in principle but technically sophisticated in execution: if the elevator car moves with the landing door or car door open — in any direction, at any speed — the UCM system detects this motion and brings the car to an immediate controlled stop.

This scenario can arise from:
- Brake wear or partial brake failure.
- Control system faults sending erroneous drive commands.
- Power supply anomalies during the start of travel.

The UCM device operates independently of the main control system, with its own dedicated monitoring circuit and mechanical brake actuator.

**Overspeed Governor & Safety Gear**

If the car ever exceeds a preset maximum speed during downward travel — typically 115% of the rated speed — a centrifugal governor mounted in the machine room or hoistway engages. This mechanically trips a bi-directional safety gear clamped to the guide rails, bringing the car to a controlled stop independent of the traction drive.

Modern TekniX systems employ **bi-directional safety gears** that provide protection against both overspeed descent *and* overspeed ascent — a requirement of contemporary safety standards.

### 2. Door Safety Systems

**Infrared Light Curtain**

The landing and car doors are protected by a dense grid of infrared beams spanning the full height of the door opening. Any obstruction — a hand, a bag, a foot — that interrupts any beam triggers immediate door reversal.

Light curtains have replaced earlier mechanical door edges as the standard because they:
- Detect obstacles without requiring physical contact.
- Respond in milliseconds.
- Cover the entire door height rather than a single central edge.

**Door Under-Fascia Protection**

A specially engineered fascia panel at the base of the car door provides a physical barrier against objects or feet being caught beneath the door sill during closing, an area historically outside the light curtain's detection zone.

**CARGuard Door Locking**

For environments with children or high-risk users, a secondary electromechanical lock prevents the car door from being opened manually during travel, independently of the primary door operator.

### 3. Terminal Safety Systems

**Three-Stage Terminal Limit System**

At both the top and bottom of the hoistway, three progressively aggressive limit switches are positioned:

1. **Normal terminal slowdown switch**: Signals the drive to begin deceleration.
2. **Terminal stopping switch**: Removes drive power if the car passes the first switch.
3. **Final limit switch**: Directly cuts the main contactor if both prior switches have failed.

This redundant three-stage approach ensures that a single component failure cannot result in a buffer impact.

### 4. Power Failure Response

**Automatic Rescue Device (ARD)**

When mains power is interrupted, the elevator car may stop between floors. The ARD system engages its battery pack and moves the car at reduced speed to the nearest floor, opens the doors, and allows occupants to exit — all automatically, without external intervention.

Modern ARD systems also:
- Provide indication to a remote monitoring system that rescue mode has engaged.
- Discriminate between a brief power dip and a sustained outage before engaging rescue travel.
- Self-test battery capacity at scheduled intervals and alarm if insufficient charge is detected.

**Phase Loss & Phase Reversal Protection**

Three-phase power supplies in commercial buildings can experience phase loss or incorrect phase sequencing. If the elevator drive detects either condition, it inhibits operation to prevent motor damage and unexpected car behaviour.

## Documentation for Building Specification

When specifying an elevator for your project, request the following safety documentation from the manufacturer:

- **Conformity Certificates** to applicable standards (IS 14665, EN 81 series).
- **Type Examination Certificate** for the safety gear and overspeed governor.
- **UCM Device Test Record**.
- **Load Test Certificates** at rated capacity + 25%.
- **ARD Battery Endurance Certification**.

These documents should be retained as part of the building's maintenance file and made available for any periodic inspection by statutory authorities.

## Maintenance as Safety Infrastructure

It is important to recognise that even the most sophisticated safety system degrades without proper maintenance. A comprehensive preventive maintenance programme should include:

- Monthly: Door system check, light curtain testing, car and counterweight buffer inspection.
- Quarterly: Brake wear measurement, governor rope tension, lubrication of guide rails.
- Annual: Load test, full safety gear test, ARD battery load test, terminal switch calibration.

TekniX service programmes are structured around these intervals, with digital maintenance records provided to building managers after each visit.
